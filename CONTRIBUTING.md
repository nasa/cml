# Submitting Contributions

Thank you for your interest in contributing to CML! CML is maintained by a very small team at NASA's Johnson Space
Center. We ask that you respect the maintainers' time and read these guidelines in full before submitting issues,
pull requests, or commenting in an issue or pull request (heretofore referred to as "submissions").

All submissions must be authored by a human. You are forbidden from using artificial intelligence (AI) or other
automated tooling to submit issues, pull requests, and comments.

We only accept submissions from people who are currently using CML or are evaluating the feasibility of using CML in
support of a real spaceflight program or academic study. We will not accept drive-by submissions from users who have no
identified use-case for CML, regardless of size or correctness. CML is not a sandbox for inflating your GitHub
contribution metrics; it is a critical component in simulations used to certify the flight readiness of many
NASA and private industry space systems. If we do not recognize you, you may be asked to describe your usage of CML
before we review your contribution.

These guidelines exist so that the CML maintainers' limited time is used to benefit the people who derive value
from CML. If you do not appear to be someone who actually uses CML or if your submissions appears to be automatically
generated, your submission will be closed without review. Repeated violations of this contribution guide may result
in your account being blocked from interacting with **all NASA GitHub repositories**, not just CML.

If you are a valid contributor, please see our [detailed contributing guide](https://nasa.github.io/cml/contributing/contributing.html)
for information on submitting new models or changes to existing models to CML. In the contributing guide, you can also
find our coding standards, AI usage guidelines, and information on getting developer access to the CML repository if
you have a NASA identity.
